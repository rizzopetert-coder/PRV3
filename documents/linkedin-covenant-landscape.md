## Correction — still actively employed at OneDigital

**Pete is currently employed at OneDigital (Digital Insurance LLC) as an HR Business Partner consultant.** This resolves the timing question from the first draft of this document, but not in the low-stakes direction — it resolves it toward higher stakes.

**What this changes:**

- The "Restricted Period" is defined as employment *plus* two years after. Since employment is ongoing, **every restrictive covenant in the agreement (Sections 2–6, 12, 13) is currently active** — not a future contingency, not something that may have already expired. The 2-year post-employment clock hasn't even started yet.
- **Section 8, Fiduciary Duty, is now the most directly relevant clause in the document, and it wasn't in the original analysis at all**: *"You owe a duty of care and loyalty to the Company... in addition to duties imposed upon You under applicable law."* This is a live obligation that exists independent of how "solicit" gets defined or whether a specific customer was contacted. It's a general duty question raised by building, drafting client-facing agreements for, and running BD activity for a second practice while currently employed.
- **The "Business" scope question changes meaningfully.** The first draft of this document noted a textual gap between OneDigital's defined "Business" (insurance brokerage, benefits, COBRA/payroll) and Principal Resolution's organizational-diagnostics focus. A title of **"HR Business Partner consultant"** suggests the actual day-to-day work may sit much closer to organizational/HR advisory than pure insurance brokerage — which would narrow or close that gap, not widen it. Exhibit A's "Business" definition has a catch-all: *"activities, products, and services that are the same as or similar to"* what OneDigital actually provides — worth knowing precisely what that consulting role involves.
- **Section 2 (Trade Secrets and Confidential Information) is a current, active restriction, not a post-employment one.** It prohibits using OneDigital confidential information "for any purpose other than the Company's Business" *during* employment. If any part of Principal Resolution's methodology, taxonomy, or client-pattern insight draws on what's learned or observed in the HR Business Partner role, that's a live question now, not a hypothetical.
- **Section 9 (Assignment of Rights) is also live now, not deferred.** Work product "created within the scope of Your employment" or "related to the Business" belongs to OneDigital. If any of Principal Resolution's diagnostic framework was conceived, drafted, or refined during this employment, that's worth a direct look — this is a much sharper question for someone in an HR consulting role than it would be for someone in, say, a claims-processing role.

The original "biggest missing fact" below (termination date) is now moot for timing purposes — there is no termination date yet. What replaces it as the operative open question is the actual scope and content of the current HR Business Partner consultant role, since that's what determines how much of Sections 2, 8, and 9 is actually in play.

## What the covenant agreement actually restricts

### The defined "Business" (Exhibit A.A)

Two-part definition:
- (i) Activities/products/services Digital Insurance conducted within 2 years prior to termination, **and**
- (ii) Specifically: marketing, selling, or brokering individual health insurance, group employee benefits/health insurance/investment products, and COBRA administration/payroll services.

**Principal Resolution's stated business — organizational friction diagnostics and structural advisory — is not insurance brokerage, benefits administration, or payroll/COBRA services.** Whether that textual gap matters, and how far the catch-all clause (i) reaches, is exactly the kind of question the definition creates but doesn't answer.

### Section 4 — Non-Solicitation of Customers

Prohibits soliciting a Digital Insurance **Customer** — but only ones from Pete's last 2 years there whom he personally dealt with, supervised, obtained confidential info about, or earned compensation from — **for the purpose of selling or providing products/services competitive with the Business.**

### Section 5 — Non-Solicitation of Prospective Customers

Same structure, applied to **Prospective Customers** (people Digital Insurance solicited, not just people it sold to).

### Section 6 — Non-Recruit of Employees

Prohibits soliciting/recruiting Digital Insurance employees Pete had "Material Interaction" with or supervised, *and* prohibits disclosing their names/background to third parties for recruiting purposes. Not currently implicated by the LinkedIn plan as described, but relevant if the practice ever hires from that network.

### Section 7 — Non-Disparagement

No disparaging or defamatory statements about the Company or its current/former officers, directors, shareholders, or employees. **No stated time limit** — appears to survive indefinitely, unlike the solicitation covenants.

### Section 13 — Post-Employment Activities (the only social-media-specific clause)

Two obligations, both narrow:
- Remove any reference to Digital Insurance as *current* employer from any source Pete controls, "including but not limited to" LinkedIn/Facebook/Twitter/Instagram.
- Don't represent himself as *currently employed* by Digital Insurance anywhere, including social media.

**This clause is about accurate representation of employment status. It does not, on its face, restrict posting business content, promoting a new venture, or general LinkedIn activity.**

### Section 12 — Post-Employment Disclosure

During the Restricted Period, Pete must give a copy of *this covenant agreement* to any person/entity he works for or consults for as owner, partner, joint venturer, employee, or independent contractor — and authorizes Digital Insurance to do the same. **If still inside the Restricted Period, this is a standalone, concrete action item independent of the LinkedIn question** — it would apply to Principal Resolution engagements generally, not just LinkedIn.

### Section 9 — Assignment of Rights (IP)

Work product created during employment, based on/related to the Business, or made using company resources belongs to Digital Insurance. Given the subject-matter gap between insurance brokerage and organizational diagnostics, likely low overlap — but if any Principal Resolution methodology was drafted or conceived while still employed there, this is a category worth having counsel glance at.

### A term that is used but never defined

**"Solicit" / "solicitation" does not appear in the Exhibit A definitions.** Exhibit A defines eleven terms (Business, Confidential Information, Customer, Employee, Intellectual Property Rights, Licensed Materials, Material Interaction, Prospective Customer, Restricted Period, Trade Secrets, Work Product) — "solicit" isn't one of them, despite being the operative verb in Sections 4, 5, and 6. This is the exact ambiguity your own prior notes flagged as needing an attorney read ("definition of solicitation under agreement") — confirmed here as a real gap in the document, not just a hunch.

## What the engagement agreement's Section 3 currently says

**"Independence"** — three short paragraphs: the Practice is independent; the engagement exists "entirely outside of, and uninfluenced by, any prior or parallel corporate affiliations of the practitioner or the Client"; the Client represents the engagement is independent and not connected to any other organizational relationship; the Practice represents its advice is free of conflicts from other affiliations and will disclose any that arise.

**No entity is named.** The language is categorical — "any prior or parallel corporate affiliations" — by design, consistent with the shadow model (no personal name, no affiliated-entity reference anywhere in the document).

**The section's own embedded attorney-review flag already asks the load-bearing question:**
> "whether categorical independence language is sufficient given the practitioner's non-solicitation obligations, or whether specific entity naming is required."

In other words — the draft itself, as written by this project, already identifies this exact tension as unresolved. It doesn't reference Digital Insurance or the covenant agreement anywhere in its text; it's a general disclaimer that anticipates a problem without naming it.
