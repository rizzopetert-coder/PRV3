## The question

Does individual coaching fit naturally as a service offering alongside PRV3's four resolution services (People Tactics and Strategy, Training & Development, Intervention, Executive Advisory), and if so, how should it be positioned, governed, and bounded?

## Governing decision

**Individual coaching does not feel at odds with the model.** It is nested as a **delivery mode within Executive Advisory** — not a fifth named service. This broadens what one buying motion covers rather than requiring a separate pitch, separate positioning, or separate instrument.

## Governing principle: "org always wins"

When an organization commissions individual coaching for one of its leaders, the org's interest governs. The large majority of coaching engagements are expected to be org-commissioned (vs. an individual leader seeking coaching independently). This is the load-bearing governance rule for everything downstream — confidentiality boundary, reporting structure, and scope.

## No new instrument required

The existing 47-state taxonomy is applied through a coaching lens, drawing on existing org-level diagnosis. This is structurally consistent with the existing clinical boundary (`web/data/taxonomy.ts` has zero imports from `engine/`; engine weights never reach the self-selection surface) — coaching doesn't introduce a parallel diagnostic system, just a different delivery context for the same diagnostic content. This was flagged early as a guardrail against scope drift, and held.

## Confidentiality boundary — resolved

**Mechanism:** A fixed-category, open-content reporting template, pre-disclosed to the individual leader before the engagement begins. This removes per-session judgment calls by disclosing the boundary itself in advance, rather than requiring Pete to make an ad hoc call about what's reportable after each session.

**Candidate report categories** (fixed and pre-disclosed; content within each category stays open to practitioner judgment):
- Engagement status / cadence
- Themes, at the taxonomy/condition level (not session-level specifics)
- Progress vs. stated goals
- A flagged-item field, for anything rising to the level of an organizational condition (i.e., something that belongs in the org-level diagnostic conversation, not just the individual's)

The individual leader knows, before the first session, exactly what categories of information flow back to the org sponsor. What they say within those categories is still filtered through practitioner judgment — the boundary is structural (which categories exist) not content-level (what gets said about each one).

## Standing copy rule (locked, applies broadly)

**"Coaching" is usable only as a verb describing an activity — never as a noun or adjective labeling the service.** Not "a coaching engagement," not "executive coaching," not "our coaching service." Reason: a noun/adjective label implies ICF certification or guaranteed outcomes the practice doesn't actually claim. This rule applies to:
- Site copy
- BD materials
- The eventual engagement agreement
- All current and future content describing this delivery mode
