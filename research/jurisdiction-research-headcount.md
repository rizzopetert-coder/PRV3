# Multi-State Coverage-Threshold, Harassment Carve-Out, and Damages-Cap Survey: State Anti-Discrimination/Disability Laws (Ex-California)

## TL;DR
- **At least eight states plus DC match or exceed California's employee-friendly posture** — several (NJ, MI, MN, OR, DC, plus CT since Oct. 1, 2022 and IL since July 1, 2020) cover employers down to a single employee, and several impose no damages cap; the four flagged priority states (NY, MA, IL, WA) all have below-federal thresholds and stronger-than-federal remedies.
- **Harassment-specific "no-floor" carve-outs like California's are rarer than the general low thresholds** — confirmed beyond CA in New York (all employers), Arizona (sexual harassment, all employers), and Maryland (1+ employee for harassment). Massachusetts and Washington do NOT drop their thresholds for harassment.
- **Uncapped damages regimes exist well beyond CA** (NY, NJ, WA, MI, MN, OR, DC, MA), while several states impose their OWN caps distinct from the federal structure (Missouri and Maryland use federal-style tiers; Colorado, Arkansas, Maine defer to size-based caps; Idaho and Kansas use small fixed caps).

## Key Findings

### Priority-Four Deep Dive
**New York (NYSHRL, N.Y. Exec. Law §§ 292, 296, 297)** — Coverage: 4+ employees for general discrimination (given/confirmed); expanded to ALL employers effective Feb. 8, 2020. Harassment carve-out: YES — all employers of any size, regardless of headcount, for sexual harassment since 2018 and for all harassment/discrimination since 2020. Damages: UNCAPPED compensatory AND punitive damages after the 2019 amendments (S.6577/A.8421, signed by Gov. Cuomo Aug. 12, 2019, eff. Oct. 11, 2019); the bill amended NYSHRL § 297 to authorize punitive damages as a remedy for all NYSHRL discrimination, harassment, and retaliation claims against private employers, with no dollar ceiling specified. **CONFLICT RESOLVED:** A 2019 Paychex article stated punitive damages are "capped at $10,000." That figure is the pre-2019 **housing-discrimination-only** administrative punitive cap under former § 297(4)(c); as Paul Weiss's contemporaneous analysis put it, housing "cases will remain capped at $10,000, but the Act does not specify any cap for punitive damages in employment discrimination cases." Employment punitive damages are uncapped. CONFIRMED.

**Massachusetts (M.G.L. c. 151B, § 4)** — Coverage: 6+ employees. Harassment carve-out: NO — the 6-employee threshold applies to harassment (including sexual harassment under § 3A) just as to other discrimination; employees of employers with fewer than 6 must instead use the Massachusetts Equal Rights Act (MERA) as an alternative avenue (the SJC has confirmed this alternative). Damages: UNCAPPED — Chapter 151B allows compensatory and punitive damages with no statutory cap. In *Haddad v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc.*, 455 Mass. 91 (2009), the SJC reinstated the jury's $1 million punitive award (plus $972,774 compensatory) under c. 151B, § 4(1), holding punitive damages require "a heightened finding beyond mere liability and also beyond a knowing violation of the statute." NOTE: A pending SJC case, *Fontaine v. Philip Morris* (argued Nov. 5, 2025), could impose common-law punitive-damages guardrails — flag as an active development. CONFIRMED (threshold, no carve-out, uncapped); PARTIAL on future punitive-damages doctrine.

**Illinois (Illinois Human Rights Act, 775 ILCS 5/)** — Coverage: 1+ employee for essentially all protected categories since July 1, 2020 (Public Act 101-0430, amending the "employer" definition from 15+ to 1+). Harassment carve-out: The IHRA always covered sexual harassment (and disability/pregnancy discrimination) at 1+ even before 2020, so a harassment carve-out effectively existed and is now subsumed because the whole Act is 1+. CONFLICT FLAG: One law-firm source (Nolan Law) states race-discrimination claims under the IHRA still require 15+ employees; this appears to predate the 2020 amendment and conflicts with the IDHR's own current 1+ position — treat the 1+ figure as controlling but note the discrepancy. Damages: UNCAPPED compensatory/emotional-distress damages; PUNITIVE DAMAGES ARE NOT AVAILABLE under the IHRA. CONFIRMED (threshold, uncapped comp, no punitive).

**Washington (WLAD, RCW ch. 49.60; coverage at RCW 49.60.040 / WAC 162-16-220)** — Coverage: 8+ employees. Harassment carve-out: NO — the 8-employee threshold applies to sexual harassment/hostile work environment claims as well; only the anti-retaliation provision (RCW 49.60.210) applies regardless of size. Damages: UNCAPPED compensatory and punitive; the Washington Supreme Court in *Blakely v. City of Vancouver* rejected federal-style caps, and WLAD permits unlimited compensatory damages. CONFIRMED (threshold, no carve-out, uncapped compensatory).

### Notably Low Coverage Thresholds (comparable to NY's 4 or CA's 5)
Employers covered at **1 employee / any size**: New Jersey, Michigan, Minnesota, Oregon, Hawaii, Colorado, Connecticut (since Oct. 1, 2022), Illinois (since July 1, 2020), Wisconsin, Vermont, Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota, Maine, and the District of Columbia. At **2**: Wyoming, and (per one source) Alaska. At **4**: New York, Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, New Mexico, Delaware (general). At **5**: California, Idaho.

### Additional Harassment No-Floor Carve-Outs Beyond CA
Confirmed: New York (all employers), Arizona (sexual harassment, all employers), Maryland (1+ employee for harassment vs. 15+ general), Illinois (sexual harassment historically 1+). For states already at 1+/all-size general coverage, a separate harassment carve-out is moot.

### Additional Uncapped / Unusually Structured Damages Beyond CA
- **Uncapped**: New York, New Jersey (NJLAD itself uncapped), Washington, Michigan (uncapped compensatory, but no punitive), Minnesota (uncapped, plus treble and — since 2024 — uncapped punitive), Oregon (uncapped noneconomic per Oregon Supreme Court 2021), Massachusetts, District of Columbia.
- **Own tiered caps distinct from federal**: Missouri (SB 43, signed by Gov. Greitens June 30, 2017, eff. Aug. 28, 2017: exclusive of attorneys' fees, back pay plus $50,000 for >5 but <100 employees; $100,000 for >100 but <200; $200,000 for >200 but <500; and $500,000 for 500+); Maryland (§ 20-1009(b)(3)/§ 20-1013: $50k/$100k/$200k/$300k by size).
- **Size-based caps mirroring/deferring to federal**: Colorado, Arkansas, Maine (for employers with 15+).
- **Small fixed caps**: Idaho ($1,000 per willful violation, punitive); Kansas ($2,000 for pain, suffering, and humiliation).

### Flagged Recent Legislative Changes (last ~2–3 years)
- **Connecticut**: threshold lowered from three employees to one or more, eff. Oct. 1, 2022 (Public Act 22-82, CGS § 46a-51(10)) — now the lowest in the nation.
- **Illinois**: threshold dropped 15→1, eff. July 1, 2020 (P.A. 101-0430).
- **Colorado**: POWR Act (SB 23-172), eff. Aug. 7, 2023 — rejected "severe or pervasive" harassment standard, added marital status.
- **Minnesota**: the 2024 amendment (HF4109, signed by Gov. Walz May 15, 2024) completely removed the $25,000 cap on punitive damages except for claims against political subdivisions; no cap applies to private-employer cases filed on or after Aug. 1, 2024, and harassment beyond sexual harassment was clarified.
- **Michigan**: 2023 ELCRA amendment added sexual orientation/gender identity.

## Recommendations
1. **Treat the priority-four states plus NJ, MI, MN, OR, CO, CT, and DC as "California-tier" exposure zones.** For diagnostic scoring, group states by (a) threshold ≤1 employee, (b) uncapped damages, and (c) harassment carve-out. States hitting all three or two-of-three (NY, NJ, DC; MI/MN/OR/WA/MA on damages) warrant the highest small-employer risk flag.
2. **Immediately correct any inherited assumption that the NYSHRL punitive cap is $10,000** — it is uncapped; the $10,000 figure was housing-only and pre-2019.
3. **Verify the flagged conflicts before publishing client-facing figures**: Alaska (1+ vs 2+), West Virginia (12 vs 15), Illinois race-discrimination threshold (15 vs 1), and Virginia's tiered thresholds. Pull the primary statute text for each.
4. **Re-pull damages-cap data via primary sources for all PARTIAL states** (most secondary-source-only entries), especially Texas, Tennessee, Florida, and Colorado where state-specific cap structures exist but were not confirmed against statute here.
5. **Monitor active 2025–2026 reforms**: Massachusetts (*Fontaine* punitive-damages case), and any further threshold reductions echoing CT/IL. Benchmark that would change recommendations: any state dropping its threshold to 1 or removing its damages cap.

## Caveats
- **Aggregator reliance**: Threshold values for roughly a dozen smaller states rest primarily on two attorney-compiled 50-state surveys (Justia; Blanchard & Walker) rather than primary statutory text pulled this session; these are marked PARTIAL or "secondary source." A wrong figure is worse than a flagged gap, so these should be statute-verified before use in a paid product.
- **Stale-source risk**: The Justia survey lists Connecticut at 3+ (now 1+ since Oct. 2022), demonstrating that even good aggregators lag legislative change. Assume any un-dated aggregator figure may predate 2022–2024 reforms.
- **Harassment carve-outs**: "Not found" does NOT mean "no carve-out exists" — it means no carve-out was confirmed this session. Only CA, NY, AZ, MD (and historically IL) were affirmatively confirmed.
- **Damages caps**: Many entries reflect general remedy availability, not a confirmed statutory cap figure; several administrative-only regimes (Alaska, Utah, Wisconsin) sharply limit compensatory/punitive damages in ways not fully captured here.
- **Administrative vs. court forums**: Several states cap or bar certain damages only in the administrative forum while permitting more in court (or vice versa); this axis was not exhaustively mapped per state.
