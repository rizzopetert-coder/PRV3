### change-management-failure-rate

**1. CURRENT FINDING**

This claim was flagged in Session 51 as pending independent verification. Research now establishes the following with high confidence from primary and peer-reviewed sources:

**Origin of the "70%" figure:**

The figure is not empirically sourced. Its traceable genealogy is:

- **Hammer & Champy (1993):** *Reengineering the Corporation.* 
The original passage reads: "Our unscientific estimate is that as many as 50 per cent to 70 per cent of the organizations that undertake a reengineering effort do not achieve the dramatic results they intended."
 The authors themselves called it an "unscientific estimate," and Hammer subsequently clarified in 1995 that 
the statement "has been widely misrepresented and transmogrified and distorted into a normative statement. There is no inherent success or failure rate for reengineering."


- **Beer & Nohria (2000):** *Harvard Business Review,* "Cracking the Code of Change." 
Beer and Nohria stated "The brutal fact is that about 70% of all change initiatives fail," with no footnote or supporting evidence for this claim.


- **Kotter:** 
Kotter wrote that he estimated from his years of study that "more than 70% of needed change…fails," again without citing empirical data.


- **McKinsey (2021):** 
McKinsey's own 2021 report "Losing from Day One" includes a footnote citing Kotter — meaning McKinsey cites Kotter, who provided no primary data.
 Each source in the citation chain traces back to a prior popularizer rather than original empirical research.

**Peer-reviewed assessment:**


Hughes (2011), published in the *Journal of Change Management* (Vol. 11, No. 4), systematically examined the five most prominent published sources for the 70% claim — Hammer and Champy, Beer and Nohria, a Bain & Company article, a McKinsey article, and Kotter — and concluded: "Whilst the existence of a popular narrative of 70 per cent organizational change failure is acknowledged, the absence of valid and reliable empirical evidence to support such a narrative is highlighted."
 This is the most rigorous peer-reviewed treatment of the claim's origins and remains the definitive assessment as of the search date.
Source: University of Brighton research repository — https://research.brighton.ac.uk/en/publications/do-70-per-cent-of-all-organizational-change-initiatives-really-fa/

**More recent figures (2024):**


The 70% statistic lacks valid empirical evidence, as Hughes (2011) demonstrated systematically; however, more recent consultancy studies show figures in a similar range: Bain cites 88% (2024) and Mutaree cites 77% (2024).


The Bain (2024) figure has a primary source: 
Bain & Company's April 15, 2024 press release reports that new research from the firm shows only about 12% of organizations achieve their original transformation ambitions — yielding the cited 88% failure figure. Bain identifies the strongest predictor of success as how well the company retains, develops, and acquires the right talent and capabilities.

Source: https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2024/88-of-business-transformations-fail-to-achieve-their-original-ambitions-those-that-succeed-avoid-overloading-top-talent/

**Critical distinction:** The Bain 2024 figure is a consultancy self-reported survey result (methodology not fully disclosed in the press release), not a peer-reviewed study. Its scope is "business transformations" broadly, not organizational change initiatives specifically. It should not be treated as a validated replacement for the 70% figure — it is a different instrument with the same methodological limitations.

One secondary source summarizes the state of actual empirical evidence bluntly: 
"Not only is there no evidence that 70% of change programmes fail but what little evidence there is suggest that the failure rate is more like 6%"
 — though this counter-figure similarly lacks disclosed primary sourcing and should be treated with the same skepticism.

### dol-back-wages-and-mechanism

**1. CURRENT FINDING**

**Dollar figure:** 
The DOL Wage and Hour Division's January 8, 2026 press release announces it recovered more than $259 million in back wages for nearly 177,000 employees nationwide — an average of $1,465 per worker — in fiscal year 2025.
 
This is the highest recovery of back wages since 2019.


**Mechanism — liquidated damages:** 
On June 27, 2025, the DOL announced a significant shift in its wage and hour enforcement policy, ending the longstanding practice of seeking liquidated damages in administrative, pre-litigation proceedings under the FLSA. This move, outlined in Field Assistance Bulletin (FAB) No. 2025-3, represents a marked change in regulatory approach.
 
Pursuant to FAB 2025-3, the WHD is no longer permitted to seek liquidated damages in any pre-litigation investigation or resolution unless a private lawsuit is filed by an employee or the Secretary of Labor. Instead, WHD settlements are now limited to recovering unpaid minimum wages and overtime compensation.


**PAID program reinstatement:** 
The PAID program was first introduced during President Donald Trump's first term and abandoned during the Biden administration. On July 24, 2025, the DOL announced it was (re)launching the PAID program. Notably, almost a month earlier, on June 27, 2025, WHD issued Field Assistance Bulletin No. 2025-3, announcing that WHD would "no longer request liquidated damages in any pre-litigation investigation or resolution" under the FLSA.

- Primary: DOL/WHD press release, https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/whd/whd20260108, January 8, 2026
- Primary: DOL/WHD fact sheet / annual data page, https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/data, verified current
