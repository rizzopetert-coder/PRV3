Review Clustering

Aggregated employee review language, clustered by the organizational condition the language describes rather than the surface complaint. The unfiltered voice of employees at the moment they stopped caring what the organization thought of them.

01
"Management doesn't listen"
Frequency: Extremely High — top 3 theme across all industries
Representative Language
"Leadership is out of touch."
"Feedback goes into a black hole."
"Town halls are performative."
"Nothing ever changes after the survey."
Underlying Condition
The organization has built listening infrastructure — surveys, town halls, open door policies — that produces the appearance of listening without the substance of it. The gap between signal collected and signal acted on is the condition. The listening theater is often worse than silence because it creates the expectation of response that is then violated repeatedly.
Taxonomy Verdict — New Candidate
Confirms Organizational Deafness. Distinct from The Untouchable — this is systemic, not concentrated in specific leaders. An organization can have no untouchable individuals and still be organizationally deaf.
Question Design Language
Does your organization regularly collect employee feedback? When it does, how often do you observe visible changes resulting from it? The user recognizes the pattern without knowing what's being measured.
02
"Favoritism and politics over performance"
Frequency: Extremely High — top 5 theme across all industries
Representative Language
"Who you know matters more than what you do."
"Promotions are based on visibility not results."
"If you're not in the inner circle you're invisible."
"The same people get everything regardless of performance."
Underlying Condition
The promotion and recognition architecture has decoupled from performance. Advancement is governed by relationship proximity, visibility to specific decision-makers, and political alignment rather than demonstrated capability or contribution. People who understand this adapt — optimizing for visibility over output. People who don't eventually leave.
Taxonomy Verdict — New Candidate
Partially overlaps with The Founder's Grip (favoritism concentrated around one person) and The Untouchable (political insulation of specific leaders). But favoritism as a systemic promotion architecture — distributed across the leadership layer, not concentrated — is a distinct condition with a different resolution path.
The Invisible Meritocracy The Political Ladder The Insider Track The Visibility Game
Question Design Language
When someone is promoted in your organization, what do colleagues typically attribute the decision to — performance, relationships, visibility, or a combination? The attribution pattern is the diagnostic signal.
03
"No growth or development opportunities"
Frequency: Very High — top exit reason across most industries
Representative Language
"Dead end job."
"Promised development that never materialized."
"Managers hoard their best people instead of developing them."
"I learned nothing in two years."
Underlying Condition — Two Distinct Sub-Conditions
Development Infrastructure Absence — the organization genuinely has no systematic approach to building capability. And Development Promise Violation — the organization described development opportunities in recruiting that don't exist in practice. The second produces faster and more bitter attrition because it involves a broken agreement, not just an absence.
Taxonomy Verdict — Confirms Existing + New Candidate
Development Infrastructure Absence confirmed from tool experiment. Development Promise Violation is a new candidate — a trust condition with a specific mechanism, distinct from the structural absence of development. Same surface complaint, different root condition, different resolution path.
Question Design Language
Has your organization made specific commitments to your development that have not been delivered? Was this discussed before you joined or after? The timing is the diagnostic signal — before joining is promise violation, after joining is infrastructure failure.
04
"Work-life balance is nonexistent"
Frequency: Very High — accelerated post-2020
Representative Language
"They say they value balance but reward overwork."
"Heroes are celebrated, sustainability is penalized."
"The workload is impossible and nobody acknowledges it."
"Burnout is the culture."
Underlying Condition
The organization has a stated culture of balance and an operational culture of overwork that coexist without resolution — because the people who model overwork are the people who get promoted. The condition is not the workload. It is the gap between the stated value and the rewarded behavior, specifically around sustainability. This is Culture Drift applied to a specific dimension with enough frequency to warrant its own state.
Taxonomy Verdict — Partial Match / Extends Existing
Confirms Culture Drift as a new candidate. Also surfaces Sustainability Theater as a specific sub-condition — the organization performs concern for wellbeing while systematically rewarding the opposite. May be a severity variant of Culture Drift or a distinct state in its own right.
Question Design Language
How does your organization respond to leaders who consistently work unsustainable hours — celebrated, cautioned, or treated as neutral? The answer locates the organization on the Culture Drift severity spectrum precisely.
05
"Poor communication from leadership"
Frequency: Very High — consistent across all organization sizes
Representative Language
"We find out about changes from the rumor mill."
"Leadership makes decisions and announces them without explanation."
"Strategy is a mystery to everyone below director level."
"We're treated like children who can't handle information."
Underlying Condition
Information architecture failure. The organization has no systematic approach to communicating decisions, strategy, or change to the people who have to implement them. Distinct from Organizational Deafness — that's downward signal going unheard. This is downward communication going undelivered. Leaders believe they've communicated. Employees experience an information vacuum. The perception gap itself is the diagnostic signal.
Taxonomy Verdict — New Candidate
Distinct from Change Absorption Failure — that condition is about adoption failure. This is about information failure that precedes adoption. An organization can fail at communication without initiating change, and can fail at adoption despite good communication. Two different conditions with overlapping symptoms.
The Information Gap The Closed Door The Vacuum The Rumor Mill
Question Design Language
When your organization makes a significant decision affecting your team, how do you typically learn about it — formal leadership communication, your manager, or informal channels? The routing of information is the diagnostic signal.
06
"Toxic culture / toxic manager"
Frequency: High — most emotionally intense reviews
Representative Language
"Hostile work environment."
"Walking on eggshells."
"Bullying is tolerated."
"HR did nothing when I reported it."
"The culture protects the wrong people."
Underlying Condition — Two Distinct Sub-Conditions
Individual toxic leadership — a specific manager whose behavior is harmful and tolerated. And systemic toxicity — harmful behavior distributed across the culture and normalized. The critical diagnostic distinction: "HR did nothing when I reported it" moves this from individual leadership failure to organizational systems failure. That phrase is the signal that separates The Untouchable from something deeper — an organization where protection of bad actors is structural.
Taxonomy Verdict — Partial Match + New Candidate
Partially confirms The Untouchable. But "HR did nothing" surfaces a distinct new candidate: an organization where accountability infrastructure exists and actively fails — not because it's absent but because it protects the wrong people. This is Misconduct Infrastructure Absence at its most specific presentation.
The Protected Class The Closed System The Captured Function The Silent Partner
Question Design Language
When misconduct or inappropriate behavior is reported in your organization, what typically happens — addressed promptly, addressed inconsistently, rarely addressed, or never addressed? The answer locates the organization on the spectrum between The Untouchable and HR Capture precisely.
07
"Constant change and instability"
Frequency: High — elevated in technology, healthcare, and retail
Representative Language
"Reorganized every six months."
"Strategy changes constantly."
"Change fatigue is real — nobody believes anything will stick."
"We just implemented this and they're already changing it."
Underlying Condition
Change Absorption Failure at its most severe presentation — where accumulated failure of previous change initiatives has produced organizational cynicism so deep that new initiatives fail before they begin. The employees are not resistant to this change. They're responding rationally to a pattern of changes that didn't stick. The condition is not instability — it's the organization's loss of credibility as a change agent with its own people.
Taxonomy Verdict — Confirms New Candidate
Confirms Change Absorption Failure as a distinct state candidate from the tool experiment. The severity spectrum is clear: Emerging is change failing at adoption. Entrenched is burned credibility across multiple initiatives. Endemic is a workforce that has stopped engaging with change as a category — any announcement produces preemptive disengagement.
Question Design Language
When your organization announces a significant new initiative or direction, what is the typical initial reaction from employees — cautious optimism, skeptical wait-and-see, or immediate disengagement? The answer locates the severity tier directly without the user understanding what it's measuring.
08
"Underpaid / not valued"
Frequency: Very High — #1 stated exit reason in most surveys
Representative Language
"Below market pay."
"Discovered my colleagues are paid significantly more for the same work."
"They know I'm underpaid and don't care."
"The raises are insulting."
Underlying Condition
Compensation Incoherence in its most interpersonally experienced form. The financial gap is real. But "they know I'm underpaid and don't care" reveals something more specific: the organization is aware of the inequity and has chosen not to address it. The financial gap is the evidence. The indifference is the injury. These are two different conditions requiring two different diagnoses.
Taxonomy Verdict — Confirms Existing + Extends
Confirms Compensation Incoherence from the tool experiment. Also surfaces Compensation Indifference as a distinct Attitude liability condition dressed in compensation language. The diagnostic must distinguish between organizations that don't know they're inequitable and organizations that do know and have not acted. Different states, different resolution paths.
Question Design Language
Has your organization ever acknowledged that your compensation is below market or below peers in similar roles? If yes, what action followed that acknowledgment? The follow-up is more diagnostic than the initial question.
09
"No accountability for poor performers or bad managers"
Frequency: High — strongest predictor of high-performer attrition
Representative Language
"Mediocre people stay forever."
"High performers carry poor performers."
"Nothing happens to bad managers."
"The same people underperform year after year."
Underlying Condition
Unmanaged Underperformance — a distinct condition from The Untouchable. The Untouchable is about leaders politically insulated from accountability for their leadership behavior. This condition is about organizations that systematically fail to manage performance at any level. The accountability mechanism is broken organizationally, not just for specific individuals. The result: high performers carry underperformers, resent the organization for tolerating it, and leave.
Taxonomy Verdict — New Candidate
Partially overlaps with The Untouchable and The Unformed Leader but distinct. Unmanaged Underperformance as a systemic organizational condition — where the performance management architecture has collapsed — produces different observable signals and requires a different resolution path than either existing state.
The Unchecked Floor The Tolerance Norm The Performance Amnesty The Soft Landing
Question Design Language
In your organization, when someone consistently underperforms in their role, what typically happens — active management toward improvement, informal tolerance, or no visible response? The option selected produces the diagnostic signal without revealing the scoring logic.
10
"Micromanagement"
Frequency: High — top 10 theme across all organization sizes
Representative Language
"Managers don't trust their teams."
"No autonomy whatsoever."
"Treated like a child."
"Manager wants to be CC'd on everything."
"Great work means nothing if you didn't follow the exact process."
Underlying Condition — Two Distinct Sub-Conditions
Individual manager control behavior — a specific manager whose need for control produces micromanagement regardless of organizational context. And organizational micromanagement culture — where the authority architecture is so centralized that managers can't delegate even when they want to. The first is a leadership formation problem. The second is a structural authority problem. Identical surface complaint, categorically different root conditions.
Taxonomy Verdict — Maps to Existing + New Candidate
Individual case maps to The Unformed Leader. Structural case maps to The Founder's Grip or Decision Paralysis. But micromanagement as a distributed cultural norm — not concentrated in one person or structure — may be a distinct state. The Trust Deficit condition describes an organization that has institutionalized distrust as its operating model.
The Control Culture The Permission Organization The Trust Deficit The Approval Chain
Question Design Language
When you make a decision within what you understand to be your role, how often does it require approval from someone above you — never, sometimes, usually, or always? The frequency locates the condition between individual manager failure and structural authority failure.
11
"DEI is performative"
Frequency: High and rising — most acute in post-2020 reviews
Representative Language
"Diversity initiatives are window dressing."
"Hired for optics, not for real inclusion."
"Diverse candidates hired at entry level, never promoted."
"The leadership team looks nothing like the workforce."
"We celebrate heritage months and ignore actual equity issues."
Underlying Condition
Performative Equity — where the organization has invested in the symbols of DEI without the systems that would produce its substance. The pipeline is diverse. The leadership isn't. The reason is structural — the promotion architecture, sponsorship networks, performance criteria, and compensation systems haven't changed. The DEI investment is at the awareness layer. The barrier is at the systems layer. The gap between the two is the condition.
Taxonomy Verdict — New Candidate
Distinct from Hiring Bias (selection layer failure) — this condition is at the advancement layer. The organization hires diverse talent and fails to advance it. Significant financial consequence: organizations that fail at inclusion lose diverse talent at elevated rates and face growing regulatory exposure under OFCCP and expanding state-level requirements.
The Inclusion Gap The Pipeline Paradox The Symbolic Ceiling The Equity Facade
Question Design Language
At what level of your organization does demographic diversity become noticeably less represented — entry level, mid-level management, senior leadership, or executive? The answer locates the barrier in the advancement architecture precisely.
12
"Senior leadership is disconnected"
Frequency: High — most prevalent in mid-size and enterprise organizations
Representative Language
"Executives live in a different reality."
"They have no idea what it's like on the ground."
"Strategy makes sense in the boardroom and falls apart in execution."
"They give motivational speeches but have no idea what we actually do."
Underlying Condition
The Executive Reality Gap — distinct from The Untouchable. The executives may be held accountable. They're just not informed. The condition is perceptual and informational, not behavioral. Good intentions, bad information. The structural filters that surround senior roles — prepared briefings, curated updates, optimistic reporting chains — systematically insulate leadership from operational reality. The gap between what leadership believes is true and what employees experience as true is the condition.
Taxonomy Verdict — New Candidate
Partially overlaps with The Untouchable but meaningfully distinct. The Untouchable is indifferent by choice or inertia. The Executive Reality Gap is uninformed by structure. Different cause, different resolution path. Coaching and accountability won't fix an information architecture problem.
The Altitude Problem The Filter Layer The Executive Bubble The Ground Floor Gap
Question Design Language
When your senior leadership makes decisions affecting your day-to-day work, how often do those decisions reflect an accurate understanding of operational reality — consistently, sometimes, rarely, or almost never? The answer distinguishes the disconnected executive from the indifferent one.
13
"Promises made, promises broken"
Frequency: High — most damaging to employer brand reviews
Representative Language
"They said there would be a path to promotion."
"Was told the role would evolve — it didn't."
"They described a culture that doesn't exist."
"What they said in the interview and what I found are different organizations."
Underlying Condition
Psychological Contract Violation — where the implicit and explicit agreements made during recruiting and onboarding are not honored in practice. The injury is not just the specific broken promise. It's the trust infrastructure the organization relies on for engagement, discretionary effort, and retention. A violated psychological contract produces disengagement that looks like attitude or motivation failure but is actually a rational response to broken agreements.
Taxonomy Verdict — New Candidate
Well-documented in organizational psychology research with measurable effects on engagement, performance, and retention. Distinct from Culture Drift — that's a gradual divergence between stated and lived values. Psychological Contract Violation is a specific broken agreement at a specific moment. The financial consequence narrative is direct: research consistently finds that violated psychological contracts reduce discretionary effort by 30-40%.
The Broken Contract The Misrepresented Organization The Gap Between Offer and Reality The Bait and Switch
Question Design Language
Thinking about what you understood about this organization before you joined versus what you've experienced since — how accurately was the organization represented to you? The gap between expectation and experience is the diagnostic signal, and the size of the gap determines severity.
14
"HR is useless / HR protects the company not employees"
Frequency: High — present in every industry, most acute in manufacturing, hospitality, retail
Representative Language
"HR is there to protect the company, not you."
"Don't bother going to HR."
"Reported to HR, nothing happened, then I was managed out."
"HR is a rubber stamp for whatever management wants."
Underlying Condition
HR Capture — where the HR function has lost its independence and operates as an extension of management rather than as an organizational conscience. Distinct from Misconduct Infrastructure Absence — in that condition, the infrastructure simply doesn't exist. HR Capture is when the infrastructure exists and has been compromised. The function is present, credentialed, and staffed. It is not independent. You can't fix this by adding HR resources. You fix it by restoring the function's independence — which is a governance problem, not a staffing problem.
Taxonomy Verdict — New Candidate
High financial consequence — a captured HR function produces exactly the liability environment that employment litigation thrives in. It's also the condition most likely to produce the news coverage that destroys employer brands. The organizational condition underneath every major workplace misconduct headline is usually HR Capture. This is a high-priority new candidate.
The Captured Function The Company HR The Compromised Partner The Silent Watchdog
Question Design Language
When an employee has a concern about their manager or leadership, how would you describe your HR department's likely response — employee advocate, neutral mediator, management-aligned, or unclear? The framing of the question reveals the employee's model of HR's role, which is the diagnostic signal.

Key Findings for PRV3
The taxonomy is growing in the right direction
The top-down approach surfaced conditions a consultant walks into. The review clustering surfaced conditions employees experience before anyone calls a consultant. Together they cover the full diagnostic landscape — the presenting complaint layer and the underlying condition layer simultaneously. The instrument needs to be able to reach both.
HR Capture is the highest-priority new candidate
An organization with a captured HR function is pre-disposed to every other liability condition in the taxonomy. It's the condition that makes The Untouchable possible, that allows The Unformed Leader to persist, that lets Misconduct Infrastructure Absence go unaddressed. HR Capture is upstream of more states than any other new candidate surfaced so far. It deserves early placement in the taxonomy and a prominent financial consequence narrative anchored to employment litigation data.
The instrument needs to distinguish sub-conditions with identical surface language
Multiple themes produced two distinct underlying conditions beneath a single surface complaint. "No growth opportunities" is either infrastructure failure or promise violation. "Toxic manager" is either individual leadership failure or systemic protection of bad actors. "Micromanagement" is either formation failure or structural authority failure. The question design must be precise enough to distinguish these pairs — because the resolution path diverges significantly for each.
The Executive Reality Gap is not The Untouchable
This distinction matters for the private output. An executive who is politically insulated from accountability needs a confrontation. An executive who is structurally insulated from operational reality needs better information architecture and closer proximity to the work. Coaching and accountability will not fix an information problem. These are different states requiring different interventions — and the diagnostic must be precise enough to tell them apart.
