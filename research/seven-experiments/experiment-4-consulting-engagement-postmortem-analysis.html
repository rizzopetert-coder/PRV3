Consulting Engagement
Postmortem Analysis

The most self-referential experiment in the series — and the most directly relevant to Principal Resolution's positioning. Where the other experiments asked "what conditions exist in organizations," this experiment asks "what conditions make organizations unable to resolve their own conditions even with outside help." That's a different question. And the answer is what separates a diagnostic that produces a report from a diagnostic that produces change.

If a consulting engagement fails, the reason is almost never that the recommendations were wrong. The reasons are organizational. Understanding those conditions is the most direct path to PRV3's positioning — because the diagnostic that identifies the conditions that block resolution before the engagement begins is worth more than the engagement itself.

Failure Pattern
The Engagement That Produces a Report Nobody Acts On
Frequency
Most common consulting failure mode
Surface Reason
"Priorities shifted" / "timing wasn't right"
Actual Conditions
3 distinct organizational conditions
How the Failure Presents
The engagement is conducted thoroughly. Findings are accurate. Recommendations are sound. The report is delivered. Nothing changes. This failure mode is so common it has produced an entire consulting sub-industry — implementation consulting — whose existence confirms that the gap between recommendation and action is a reliable organizational condition, not an occasional oversight.
What It Reveals
The report-to-action gap almost never results from disagreement with recommendations. In post-engagement interviews, leaders who didn't implement recommendations almost always agree they were right. The gap results from one of three organizational conditions — each distinct, each requiring a different resolution path, each visible to a sufficiently precise diagnostic.
PRV3 Implication
The PRV3 diagnostic should surface all three conditions before an engagement recommendation is made — because an engagement delivered to an organization in Implementation Courage Deficit will produce the same outcome as every prior engagement they haven't implemented. The diagnostic earns its value before the engagement begins.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Decision Authority Ambiguity
Authority is clear within functions and absent across them. The recommendation requires cross-functional action. Nobody has authority across domains. The recommendation doesn't fail to get decided — it fails to find an owner who can implement it. Distinct from Decision Paralysis — this is cross-functional mandate gap, not general authority ambiguity.
New Candidate
Recommendation Absorption Deficit
The organization lacks internal capacity to implement the recommendation without continued external involvement. The authority exists. The capability doesn't. Organizations that chronically produce this pattern have substituted external expertise for internal capability development — using consulting to produce outputs they should generate internally.
New Candidate — High Priority
Implementation Courage Deficit
The recommendation requires doing something politically difficult or culturally contrary. Leadership knows it. They won't act on it because acting requires confronting something the organization has invested in avoiding. The most common consulting failure mode that practitioners discuss least openly — because it implicates the client rather than the engagement.

Failure Pattern
The Engagement That Gets Cancelled or Descoped
Surface Reason
"Budget constraints"
Timing Signal
Cancellation follows specific diagnostic event
Actual Conditions
3 distinct organizational conditions
How the Failure Presents
The engagement begins with broad mandate and genuine organizational commitment. Midway through, scope is reduced, timeline is compressed, or the engagement is terminated before findings are delivered. Budget is the stated reason. The organizational condition is almost never budget alone. The timing of the budget constraint — almost always following a specific diagnostic event — is the signal.
What It Reveals
Cancellation or descoping follows a specific pattern: a difficult interview, an unexpected finding in early data, a preliminary readout that produces discomfort. The budget constraint materializes immediately after that event. This is not coincidence — it is the organization's self-protective response to a diagnostic process that is surfacing something it wasn't prepared to see.
PRV3 Implication
Anxiety Threshold Breach is the organizational condition that the PRV3 instrument's framing must navigate. A diagnostic that announces its depth of investigation produces higher Anxiety Threshold risk than one that allows the verdict to emerge from the user's own responses. The P-03 design principle — the instrument rule that diagnostic questions observe conditions while users recognize the result, with scoring weight invisible to the respondent — is specifically designed to reduce Anxiety Threshold Breach by making the diagnostic feel less threatening than it is.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Anxiety Threshold Breach
The diagnostic process surfaces something the organization wasn't prepared to see. The organization stops the process before findings are formally delivered — to prevent them from becoming organizational knowledge. Distinct from Implementation Courage Deficit (which occurs after delivery) — this occurs before delivery, when the organization senses what the findings will be.
New Candidate
Leadership Continuity Risk in Change Initiatives
Change initiatives dependent on specific individual champions whose departure terminates the initiative. The organization can start change. It can't sustain change through leadership transition. The new leader inherits an engagement they didn't commission, investigating questions they didn't ask, likely to produce findings that implicate the predecessor.
New Candidate
Stakeholder Capture
Engagement scope progressively narrowed by stakeholders who perceive findings as threatening. The engagement begins investigating the system; it ends investigating a narrow symptom that doesn't implicate any current power structure. The findings are accurate within their narrowed scope and irrelevant to the actual condition.
3
Failure Pattern
The Engagement That Produces Change That Doesn't Stick
Timeline
Regression at 6–18 months post-engagement
Surface Reason
"Leadership attention shifted"
Most Important Finding
Structural Reassertion — explains most change failure
How the Failure Presents
The engagement is completed. Recommendations are implemented. Behavior changes in the short term. Six to eighteen months later, the organization has returned to its prior condition. Leadership attributes this to implementation failure, consultant quality, or employee resistance. Almost none of these attributions are accurate. The failure is almost always structural.
What It Reveals
Change that doesn't stick fails at one of three layers: the structural layer (systems that govern behavior weren't changed), the cultural layer (norms that govern acceptability weren't changed), or the accountability layer (mechanisms that sustain change weren't built). Of the three, the structural layer is the most common and the least recognized — because structural systems are invisible compared to behavioral change, which is visible and attributable.
PRV3 Implication
Structural Reassertion explains why most consulting engagements that address behavioral conditions produce temporary results. The PRV3 private output for any condition should include the structural systems that sustain it — not just because this is honest diagnosis, but because naming the structural layer is what makes the resolution path specific enough to produce lasting change.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate — Most Important Finding
Structural Reassertion
Organizational systems — performance management, compensation, role design, reporting structures — designed to produce the behaviors the organization is attempting to change. The systems win over time. Without changing the systems, behavioral interventions produce temporary change that the systems absorb and reverse. The mechanism underneath Training-Behavior Gap and Development Without Stakes.
Confirms and Explains
Training-Behavior Gap
Confirmed. The gap exists because structural systems reassert the behaviors they were designed to produce. Coaching operates at the behavioral layer. Structural Reassertion operates at the systems layer. The systems layer always wins over time.
Confirms and Explains
Development Without Stakes
Confirmed. Development without stakes is the specific accountability layer failure — the structural mechanism that would sustain behavior change (consequences for non-change) was never built into the organizational system.
4
Failure Pattern
The Engagement Where the Findings Are Rejected
Frequency
Less common, more instructive
Surface Reason
"Methodology was flawed"
Actual Conditions
3 distinct organizational conditions
How the Failure Presents
The engagement is completed. Findings are delivered. The client organization rejects the findings as inaccurate, biased, or methodologically unsound. Rarely results from actual methodological problems. Findings are rejected when they conflict with the organization's existing understanding of itself — its narrative, its identity, or the self-perception of its leaders.
What It Reveals
The rejection mechanism is the organizational psychology single-loop learning failure — the organization can accept findings that fit the existing narrative; it cannot accept findings that require the narrative to change. Double-loop learning would require questioning the narrative itself. The organization hasn't developed that capacity. The findings arrive into a narrative that is stronger than the evidence.
PRV3 Implication
The PRV3 instrument's design specifically reduces Narrative Lock risk — because the user supplies the situational responses and the engine infers the verdict, the user cannot easily reject findings as externally imposed. The verdict emerges from the user's own language embedded precisely in the output. This is the P-02 design principle — the instrument rule that the output must name the verdict the user was already circling without having named it themselves — working as a defense against Narrative Lock.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Narrative Lock
The organization's dominant internal narrative about its own culture, leadership quality, and organizational health is too strongly held to be updated by contradicting evidence. The findings don't change the narrative — the narrative absorbs the findings as a methodology artifact. Single-loop self-perception applied to organizational identity.
New Candidate
Diagnostic Fatigue
Sufficient prior diagnostic and consulting processes without outcomes have produced a cultural immune response. The findings arrive into a culture that has learned findings don't produce action. The cultural expectation becomes self-fulfilling. Recidivism Risk expressed as a cultural condition rather than a claims pattern.
Confirms and Extends
The Untouchable (as Leader Fragility)
Confirmed. When findings implicate a specific leader with sufficient organizational standing to reject them without consequence, The Untouchable condition is operating at the diagnostic level — the same political insulation that protects behavior protects against accountability for that behavior when named externally.
5
Failure Pattern
The Engagement That Succeeds in the Wrong Place
Recognition
Least recognized as a failure
Most Important Finding
Presenting Complaint Displacement — names PRV3's core value
PRV3 Relevance
Highest of any failure pattern
How the Failure Presents
The engagement produces change in the area investigated and misses the actual condition the organization needed to address. The organization presents with a leadership problem; the root condition is structural. The engagement addresses the leadership. The structure continues producing leadership failures. New leaders exhibit the same behaviors as their predecessors. The organization concludes it hired wrong again.
What It Reveals
The presenting complaint and the root condition are in different locations. The displacement follows predictable patterns: organizations present with leadership problems when the root condition is structural; they present with culture problems when the root condition is incentive misalignment; they present with communication problems when the root condition is psychological safety collapse. The pattern is not random — it is systematic.
PRV3 Core Value Proposition
The PRV3 diagnostic is specifically designed to resolve Presenting Complaint Displacement — to reach the root condition rather than the presenting complaint. Every other consulting service addresses the presenting complaint. PRV3 addresses the condition underneath it. This is the most precise statement of PRV3's positioning advantage that the series has produced.

PRV3 Core Value — New Candidate
Presenting Complaint Displacement
The presenting complaint that brings an organization to seek help is systematically displaced from the root condition producing it. The organization has correctly identified that something is wrong. It has incorrectly identified what that thing is. The displacement follows predictable patterns: leadership problems mask structural conditions; culture problems mask incentive misalignment; communication problems mask psychological safety collapse.
Confirms Across Multiple States
Displacement Patterns Confirmed
The Unformed Leader is frequently the presenting complaint when Structural Reassertion is the root condition. Silosolation (the cross-functional coordination failure state) is frequently the presenting complaint when Misaligned Incentives is the root condition. The Fracture is frequently the presenting complaint when Organizational Moral Drift is the root condition. The taxonomy now has both the presenting complaint and the root condition for each pattern.

Failure Pattern
The Engagement That Succeeds and Produces No Lasting Organizational Learning
Recognition
Recognized as success — isn't
Long-term Outcome
Same or related condition reproduced in time
PRV3 Positioning
Diagnostic Dependency names the opportunity
How the Failure Presents
The engagement succeeds. The presenting condition is resolved. The organization returns to its prior operating mode and, in time, reproduces the same or a related condition. The organization commissions another engagement. The cycle repeats. Each engagement addresses a symptom. The organizational condition that produces the symptoms — the absence of internal diagnostic capability — is never addressed because it is never named.
What It Reveals
Engagements that succeed without producing organizational learning share one characteristic: the organization outsourced the diagnostic work rather than developing internal diagnostic capability. The condition was resolved by an external party. The organization didn't develop the capacity to detect the condition earlier, address it with its own resources, or prevent its recurrence. The engagement produced a solution. It didn't produce a learning architecture.
PRV3 Long-term Positioning
The PRV3 diagnostic, properly understood, is the instrument that begins to resolve Diagnostic Dependency — not by replacing consulting but by giving the organization a more precise understanding of its own condition than it would have developed without the instrument. The diagnostic that helps an organization understand itself more precisely is worth more over time than the diagnostic that produces a one-time report. The instrument's long-term value compounds as the organization's self-diagnostic capability improves.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Diagnostic Dependency
The organization has become dependent on external diagnosis to understand its own organizational health. Not because internal capability doesn't exist — because it has never been developed. Every prior diagnostic process was outsourced. The organization has no mechanism for generating the organizational self-knowledge that would make external diagnosis less necessary — and less expensive.
Confirms from Org Psych Experiment
Learning Architecture Failure
Confirmed. Diagnostic Dependency is Learning Architecture Failure applied specifically to organizational health diagnosis. The organization lacks the double-loop learning capacity to develop internal diagnostic capability from its consulting engagement experiences.

Key Findings for PRV3
Structural Reassertion is the most important mechanism the series has surfaced
Every consulting engagement that produces temporary change and then watches the organization return to its prior condition is encountering Structural Reassertion. The structural systems — performance management, compensation, role design, reporting structures — were designed to produce the behaviors the organization is attempting to change. They are more powerful than any behavioral intervention that doesn't also change the systems. This is the mechanism underneath Training-Behavior Gap, Development Without Stakes, and most change initiative failure. The PRV3 private output for any condition should name the structural systems sustaining it.
Presenting Complaint Displacement is the most precise statement of PRV3's value
The finding that presenting complaints are systematically displaced from root conditions — and that the displacement follows predictable patterns — is the most direct articulation of what PRV3 does that other instruments don't. The organization that presents with a leadership problem and receives leadership coaching is not wrong to want the coaching. It is wrong to expect the coaching to resolve a structural condition. PRV3's value is naming the structural condition before the coaching is commissioned — or before the next engagement is commissioned into the wrong place.
Implementation Courage Deficit is the condition practitioners discuss least and encounter most
The organizational condition where leaders know what needs to be done and don't have the will to do it is the most common consulting failure mode and the least openly discussed — because it implicates the client. The PRV3 private output for this condition must be the most direct in the taxonomy. The leader already knows. The diagnostic's job is to name the avoidance and its cost. This is the magnanimous but unflinching voice doing its defining work.
