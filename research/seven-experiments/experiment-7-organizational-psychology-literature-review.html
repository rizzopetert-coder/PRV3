Organizational Psychology
Literature Review

The final experiment. Five decades of organizational psychology research analyzed for what it establishes about the conditions that produce organizational dysfunction — through what mechanism, and with what measurable effect. The explanatory layer that makes the taxonomy a diagnostic rather than a catalog.

This experiment is different in kind from the six that preceded it. The others produced conditions that are recognizable — a leader can read the description and think "that's us." The organizational psychology literature produces conditions that are explanatory — they tell you why the recognizable condition exists and persists. That explanatory layer is what makes the private output more than a diagnosis. It's what makes it a verdict.

Why This Experiment Is Different
The previous experiments identified what the conditions look like from outside — from employees, from litigants, from practitioners, from actuaries. 1
Research Construct
Role Ambiguity and Role Conflict
Foundational Research
Kahn et al. (1964)
Meta-Analysis
Gilboa et al. (2008), n=169 studies
Effect Size
Among largest in org psych literature
What the Research Establishes
Role ambiguity — the absence of clear information about what a role requires — and role conflict — incompatible demands within a role — are the two strongest predictors of job performance decline, ahead of workload, interpersonal conflict, and physical working conditions. The meta-analytic evidence across 169 studies confirms this finding across industries, cultures, and organizational types.
The Mechanism
Role ambiguity is not a personal failing — it is produced by specific structural failures: unclear job descriptions, absent performance criteria, inadequate onboarding, restructuring without role redefinition, and managers who define expectations verbally rather than documenting them. Role conflict is produced by matrix reporting structures, cultures where stated values conflict with operational demands, and performance systems that reward behaviors conflicting with stated priorities.
PRV3 Evidence
Gilboa et al. established that role ambiguity reduces performance by an average of 12–15% across the affected population. For 500 managers experiencing moderate role ambiguity, the performance cost is quantifiable before any attrition or claim calculation — a direct input to the Patterned Consequence Logic (the engine framework linking organizational conditions to quantified financial outcomes).
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Role Clarity Deficit
Neither the employee nor their manager can specify with precision what the employee is supposed to do, how performance will be evaluated, or what authority they have to act. Systemic condition produced by organizations that restructure without redefining roles, promote into expanded scope without updating expectations, or add responsibilities without removing others.
Mechanism for The Unformed Leader — the formation debt produces role ambiguity in the employee below, not just ineffective management above. The cascade is documented in the research literature.
Confirms and Explains
Decision Paralysis
Confirmed. Role conflict from competing decision-making authorities is the mechanism underneath Decision Paralysis — incompatible demands from multiple authority structures produce the paralysis the condition describes.
Confirms and Explains
Misaligned Incentives
Confirmed. Role conflict between stated organizational values and rewarded behaviors is the specific mechanism Misaligned Incentives creates at scale.
2
Research Construct
Psychological Safety
Foundational Research
Edmondson (1999)
Major Validation
Google Project Aristotle (2016), n=180 teams
Finding
Strongest predictor of high-performing teams
What the Research Establishes
Psychological safety — the shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking — is the single strongest predictor of team performance, stronger than team composition, individual talent, or technical expertise. The mechanism is informational, not attitudinal: psychologically unsafe environments suppress information, which degrades decision quality, error detection, and learning speed.
The Mechanism
Psychological safety is a team-level phenomenon produced almost entirely by leader behavior. One leader who consistently responds to concerns with defensiveness or punishment creates an unsafe environment for their entire team within weeks. The condition spreads through the organization as leaders model the behavior for their reports. The contagion pattern is downward and rapid. Recovery is slow because it requires consistent counter-evidence over extended time.
PRV3 Evidence
Psychological safety can be measured with Edmondson's validated seven-item instrument. The items are situational rather than self-referential — respondents answer about their team's behavior, not their own feelings. This means the diagnostic can detect psychological safety levels without the user recognizing what is being measured. The P-03 design principle (diagnostic questions observe conditions, users recognize the result, scoring weight invisible to the respondent) is directly satisfied.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Information Suppression Cascade
Low psychological safety has propagated upward through the management hierarchy so that information reaching senior leadership is systematically filtered. Not deliberate deception — each layer has learned not to surface problems. The Executive Reality Gap is the senior leadership experience of this condition.
Connects the Executive Reality Gap (conference experiment) to its root mechanism. The research establishes that information suppression cascades are produced by specific leader behaviors — which means the diagnostic question set can identify the cascade from observable signals without requiring the leader to self-report the filtering.
Confirms and Explains
Psychological Safety Collapse
Confirmed across exit interview, Glassdoor, and conference experiments. Now explained: the mechanism is informational, not emotional. The private output should lead with the information quality argument. The organization is making decisions on systematically incomplete data.

Research Construct
Organizational Justice
Foundational Research
Greenberg (1987)
Meta-Analysis
Cohen-Charash & Spector (2001), n=190 studies
Key Finding
Injustice predicts turnover more strongly than job satisfaction
What the Research Establishes
Organizational justice has three components: distributive (are outcomes fairly distributed), procedural (are the processes for making decisions fair), and interactional (are people treated with dignity in those processes). Injustice perceptions predict turnover intention more strongly than job satisfaction, pay satisfaction, or role clarity. The effect is consistent across cultures, industries, and organizational types.
The Critical Insight
Employees who receive fair outcomes through unfair processes report lower organizational commitment than employees who receive unfavorable outcomes through fair processes. An employee who loses a promotion through a transparent, well-documented process is more committed than an employee who receives a promotion through an opaque, arbitrary one. The process matters more than the outcome — which is the opposite of what most organizations believe.
PRV3 Evidence
Cohen-Charash and Spector (2001) meta-analysis across 190 studies: organizational injustice predicts turnover intention more strongly than job satisfaction, pay satisfaction, or role clarity. The effect size is among the largest in the turnover prediction literature. This is the financial consequence mechanism for Process Justice Failure.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Process Justice Failure
Employment decisions made through processes that employees perceive as arbitrary, opaque, or inconsistently applied. Distinct from outcome injustice — the organization may make correct decisions through indefensible processes. The research establishes that process justice failure produces organizational commitment decline and turnover intention independent of whether outcomes are actually fair.
This condition is invisible to standard engagement measurement, which asks about satisfaction with outcomes rather than confidence in processes. The PRV3 diagnostic is positioned to detect it specifically because process perception is measurable through situational questions.
Confirms and Explains
Favoritism Architecture
Confirmed. Procedural injustice is the research mechanism underneath Favoritism Architecture — the perception that advancement processes are not transparent or consistently applied is precisely what the organizational justice literature predicts and measures.

Research Construct
Conservation of Resources Theory
Foundational Research
Hobfoll (1989)
Key Principle
Resource loss spirals — loss begets loss
One of the most cited
frameworks in org stress research
What the Research Establishes
People strive to obtain, retain, protect, and foster resources — physical, psychological, social, and material. Resource loss is disproportionately stressful relative to equivalent resource gain. When resource loss occurs, people become more vulnerable to future resource loss because the defensive behaviors they adopt consume additional resources. Loss spirals are self-reinforcing: each loss makes the next loss more likely.
The Mechanism in Organizations
High performers who are losing resources invisibly adopt high-resource-consuming compensation strategies — working longer, covering for others, masking the cost — that accelerate the resource loss while maintaining visible output. The spiral ends in departure or disability. COR theory predicts that high performers leave first during resource depletion: they have the most options when their resources become unrecoverable.
PRV3 Evidence
COR theory predicts that the organization progressively loses its highest performers first during resource depletion conditions — leaving it progressively dependent on employees with fewer alternatives and lower performance ceilings. This is the financial consequence mechanism for Resource Depletion Architecture: not immediate attrition cost, but systematic degradation of the talent pool.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Resource Depletion Architecture
An organizational design that systematically depletes employee resources faster than the organization replenishes them. Not about individual overload or specific bad managers — a fundamentally extractive organizational structure. Roles expand without narrowing. Expectations increase without resource increases. The organization consumes its human capital faster than it generates it.
Explains why The Squeeze (conference experiment) and Invisible Burnout (exit interview experiment) are not random — they are the predictable outcomes of Resource Depletion Architecture. The research gives the diagnostic the causal explanation the practitioner-facing evidence couldn't provide.
Confirms and Explains
Invisible Burnout
Confirmed. COR theory is the mechanism: high performers adopt high-resource-consuming compensation strategies that maintain visible output while accelerating the underlying resource loss. The compensation strategy is what makes the burnout invisible.
Confirms and Explains
The Squeeze
Confirmed. COR theory predicts that simultaneous role expansion without resource addition produces a resource loss spiral by structural design — withdrawal from high-risk activities, defensive compliance, eventual departure.
5
Research Construct
Self-Determination Theory
Foundational Research
Deci & Ryan (1985)
Meta-Analysis
Van den Broeck et al. (2016), n=99 studies
Three Basic Needs
Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness
What the Research Establishes
Human motivation and wellbeing depend on satisfaction of three basic psychological needs: autonomy (volitional control), competence (felt effectiveness), and relatedness (connection). Need frustration — the active blocking of these needs — produces burnout, turnover intention, and counterproductive work behavior beyond the effects of need dissatisfaction alone. The distinction between frustration and dissatisfaction is critical for PRV3.
The Mechanism
Competence frustration — arbitrary or inconsistent performance feedback — shifts motivation from autonomous (I want to do this) to controlled (I have to do this to avoid punishment) to amotivated (I don't believe my effort matters). The amotivated employee is present, delivering minimum required output, and consuming organizational resources without contributing discretionary effort. This shift is stable and self-reinforcing.
PRV3 Evidence
SDT research establishes that controlled motivation produces systematically lower performance, creativity, and organizational citizenship behavior than autonomous motivation — even when the controlled and autonomously motivated employees are producing the same observable output. The performance gap is in discretionary effort and innovation, which are unmeasured by most organizational performance systems.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Motivational Architecture Failure
Reward, recognition, and performance management systems have produced a predominantly controlled or amotivated workforce. Not low engagement — a specific psychological condition where the majority of employees either perform to avoid punishment or have stopped believing their effort affects outcomes. Self-reinforcing: organizations with controlled motivation produce more controlling management.
Explains why engagement survey scores and productivity metrics can look acceptable while the organization is experiencing significant undisclosed performance degradation. The loss is in discretionary effort and innovation — unmeasured by conventional performance systems and therefore invisible until it produces a crisis.
Confirms and Explains
The Control Culture
Confirmed. SDT establishes that controlling management cultures produce autonomy frustration at scale — the mechanism underneath The Control Culture's organizational outcomes.
Confirms and Explains
Impossible Standard
Confirmed. Competence frustration through inconsistent or arbitrary performance standards is the SDT mechanism that produces amotivation — the endpoint of the Impossible Standard condition.

Research Construct
Social Identity Theory and Organizational Identification
Foundational Research
Tajfel & Turner (1979)
Key Insight
Organizational identity is a source of self-concept
Implication
Culture change is identity threat
What the Research Establishes
People derive part of their identity from group memberships and act to maintain positive distinctiveness of those groups. Organizational identification — the degree to which employees define themselves through their organizational membership — predicts organizational citizenship behavior, commitment, and resistance to organizational change. Organizational identification is produced and destroyed by organizational conditions — it is not a stable individual trait.
The Mechanism
Organizational changes that appear to threaten the organizational identity reduce identification and produce resistance that is rational from the employee's perspective — they are protecting a source of their self-concept. Culture Drift is not just a behavioral change — it is an identity threat. Post-acquisition resistance is not irrational — it is identity-protective behavior. The resistance to change that organizations label as irrational is often a predictable identity-protection response.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Identity Erosion
The organizational identity that employees joined and identified with has changed substantially enough that current employees no longer recognize themselves in the organization's stated identity. Not Culture Drift (behavioral norms) — specifically the core organizational identity: what the organization is for, what it stands for, who it serves. The employees who leave are not just disengaged — they are deidentified.
Explains the specific mechanism of post-pivot attrition: employees who joined a mission-driven organization that has become commercially-driven are not leaving because of dissatisfaction — they are leaving because the organization no longer provides the identity resource they joined it for. The private output for this condition should name this mechanism directly.
Confirms and Explains
The Second Close
Confirmed. Social identity research explains why integration efforts that acknowledge and honor the acquired organization's identity succeed more often than those that don't — reducing the identity threat reduces the resistance that produces integration failure.
Confirms and Explains
Change Absorption Failure
Confirmed. The resistance to organizational change that organizations label as irrational is often identity-protective behavior — which means change management that acknowledges identity threat will be more successful than change management that treats resistance as ignorance.

Research Construct
Moral Disengagement
Foundational Research
Bandura (1999)
Eight Mechanisms
Including euphemistic labeling, diffusion of responsibility, dehumanization
Key Insight
Produced by organizational conditions, not individual pathology
What the Research Establishes
Moral disengagement comprises eight mechanisms through which individuals disable the self-regulatory processes that normally keep behavior aligned with moral standards. These mechanisms are not individual pathologies — they are produced by organizational conditions. Organizations that structure accountability diffusely, use euphemistic language for harmful practices, and create competitive pressure that makes harmful behavior seem necessary systematically produce moral disengagement across their workforce.
The Mechanism in Organizations
Sequential Decision Blindness (from the litigation experiment) is explained by the diffusion and displacement of responsibility mechanisms — each decision-maker believes someone else holds the accountability. Normalization Creep is explained by euphemistic labeling and advantageous comparison — harmful behavior is relabeled and compared favorably to worse behavior until it becomes morally invisible. The organizational conditions produce the mechanism; the mechanism produces the behavior.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Organizational Moral Drift
Moral disengagement mechanisms embedded in the organizational culture. The language of the organization has shifted to euphemistically label harmful practices. Responsibility for consequences has been diffused across enough decision-makers that no individual feels accountable. Competitive comparison justifies practices that would otherwise be recognized as harmful. Distinct from Normalization Creep — this is systemic, not behavior-specific.
The most severe presentation of this condition produces the organizational environment in which whistleblower events, regulatory investigations, and public scandals become inevitable — because the internal moral regulation mechanism has been disabled at the organizational level. Internal Reporting Failure (litigation experiment) is the specific consequence of Organizational Moral Drift at the governance layer.
Confirms and Explains
Sequential Decision Blindness
Confirmed. The diffusion and displacement of responsibility mechanisms explain why no individual in the organization acts with retaliatory intent while the sequence constitutes retaliation. The mechanism is structural, not individual.
Confirms and Explains
Normalization Creep
Confirmed. Euphemistic labeling and advantageous comparison are the Bandura mechanisms that make harmful behavior morally invisible over time — the gradual process the Normalization Creep condition describes.

Research Construct
Psychological Contract Theory
Foundational Research
Rousseau (1989)
Meta-Analysis
Zhao et al. (2007), n=51 studies
Critical Distinction
Breach (cognitive) vs. violation (emotional)
What the Research Establishes
The psychological contract — the individual employee's beliefs about the terms of the reciprocal exchange with their employer — produces more severe responses when violated than formal contract violations, because the violation involves perceived betrayal rather than legal dispute. Psychological contract breach predicts organizational citizenship behavior decline, job satisfaction decline, and organizational commitment decline.
The Critical Distinction for PRV3
The research distinguishes breach (cognitive recognition that a promise hasn't been kept) from violation (emotional experience of betrayal). Organizations that acknowledge breach produce breach without violation. Organizations that are silent about unmet commitments produce violation. The most damaging psychological contract violations are not the largest breaches but the unacknowledged ones — because silence converts breach into violation. An organization that acknowledges and explains produces breach. Silence produces violation.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Unacknowledged Breach
Promises that haven't been kept are not acknowledged by the organization. Not deliberate deception — the organization doesn't have a mechanism for recognizing when it has made commitments it isn't fulfilling, or for communicating proactively when circumstances prevent fulfillment. Silence converts breach into violation. The most damaging psychological contract violations are not the largest breaches but the unacknowledged ones.
Explains why organizations that are "doing their best" in difficult circumstances still lose employees to trust collapse. The employees are experiencing violation, not breach — because the organization hasn't acknowledged what it couldn't deliver. The private output for this condition should distinguish the two: what you did isn't necessarily what damaged the relationship. What you didn't say is.
Confirms and Explains
Psychological Contract Violation
Confirmed across exit interview and Glassdoor experiments. The research adds the mechanism: felt violation (emotional betrayal) is the mediating variable — not the size of the breach but the organization's response to it.
Confirms and Explains
Trust Collapse
Confirmed. Trust Collapse is the accumulated endpoint of repeated Unacknowledged Breach — each silence compounds until the threshold where cognitive reassessment of the relationship produces the calm, resolved departure the exit interview experiment described.

Research Construct
Organizational Learning and Deutero-Learning
Foundational Research
Argyris & Schön (1978)
Key Distinction
Single-loop vs. double-loop vs. deutero-learning
Implication
Framework errors require double-loop correction
What the Research Establishes
Single-loop learning detects and corrects errors within existing frameworks. Double-loop learning questions the frameworks themselves when error-correction fails. Organizations incapable of double-loop learning cannot resolve fundamental dysfunctions — because fundamental dysfunctions are the product of the frameworks themselves. The organization keeps applying the same wrong solution because it cannot question the framework producing the need for a solution.
The Mechanism
Organizations that can only single-loop learn produce a specific observable pattern: persistent organizational problems that are recognized, addressed, and re-emerge unchanged. Each intervention is within the framework that generates the problem — so the problem recurs. The organization experiences this as implementation failure. It is actually framework failure. The distinction matters enormously for the resolution path: framework problems require framework change, not better implementation of the existing framework.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Learning Architecture Failure
The organization has no reliable mechanism for questioning its own frameworks when those frameworks produce persistent failure. It can correct errors (single-loop). It cannot question whether the framework producing the errors should be changed (double-loop). Produces persistent organizational problems that are recognized, addressed, and re-emerge unchanged — because each solution is within the framework generating the problem.
This condition explains why many organizations that know they have a problem cannot resolve it. The resolution path isn't "try harder" or "implement better" — it's "change the framework." The PRV3 diagnostic is positioned to name this distinction explicitly in the private output, which most consulting interventions fail to do.
Confirms and Explains
Change Absorption Failure
Confirmed and extended. Change Absorption Failure may be a single-loop learning problem (the change management approach is wrong) or a double-loop learning problem (the framework for organizational change is wrong). The distinction determines the resolution path. Argyris and Schön give the diagnostic the precision to tell them apart.
Confirms and Explains
Recidivism Risk
Confirmed. Recidivism Risk is a specific manifestation of single-loop learning failure — the organization corrected the claim outcome (settled) without questioning the framework (organizational culture, HR practices, management accountability) that produced the claim.

Research Construct
Organizational Resilience and High Reliability
Foundational Research
Weick & Sutcliffe (2001)
Source Organizations
Nuclear plants, aircraft carriers, air traffic control
Five HRO Characteristics
Preoccupation with failure, reluctance to simplify, sensitivity to operations, commitment to resilience, deference to expertise
What the Research Establishes
High Reliability Organizations operate with minimal failure despite high complexity and high risk because of five specific characteristics. The absence of each characteristic maps to specific organizational conditions already in the PRV3 taxonomy: Preoccupation with failure absent → Normalization Creep. Reluctance to simplify absent → Strategic Credibility Loss. Sensitivity to operations absent → Executive Reality Gap. Deference to expertise absent → The Founder's Grip.
The Mechanism
Organizations that don't treat near-misses as seriously as failures accumulate risk silently — the near-misses that would have triggered corrective action go unaddressed until they produce the full failure event. Organizations that simplify complex situations apply simple solutions to problems that require nuanced responses. The HRO framework describes the specific organizational architecture that prevents failure propagation — and its absence describes the architecture that allows it.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Resilience Architecture Gap
The organization has no reliable mechanism for detecting, containing, and recovering from failure before it becomes catastrophic. It recovers from each failure. It doesn't learn from them in ways that reduce future failure probability. Each recovery is a complete reconstruction from zero. The financial consequence is compounding: organizations pay the full cost of each failure rather than the reduced cost of a system designed to contain and learn from failure.
This condition explains why some organizations are perpetually in crisis — not because their leaders are incompetent but because the organization lacks the architectural capacity to convert failure experience into failure prevention. The distinction between an organization that is unlucky and one that has a Resilience Architecture Gap is detectable through the PRV3 instrument's governance and process questions.
Confirms Across Multiple States
HRO Absence Maps to Existing States
Preoccupation with failure absent → Normalization Creep and Recidivism Risk confirmed. Sensitivity to operations absent → Executive Reality Gap and Information Suppression Cascade confirmed. Deference to expertise absent → The Founder's Grip confirmed. Reluctance to simplify absent → Strategic Credibility Loss and Learning Architecture Failure confirmed.

Key Findings for PRV3
The research literature provides the "why" that makes the private output a verdict
Every other experiment produced observable conditions. This experiment produced explanatory mechanisms — the causal architecture underneath the observable conditions. The private output that says "here is what's happening" is a diagnosis. The private output that says "here is what's happening, here is why it persists, and here is what it will cost if it continues" is a verdict. The organizational psychology literature gives PRV3 the explanatory layer that produces verdicts rather than diagnoses.
The distinction between breach and violation is the most precise finding for output design
Rousseau's research on psychological contract theory produces a distinction with direct output design implications: organizations that acknowledge what they haven't delivered produce breach. Organizations that are silent produce violation. The private output for Unacknowledged Breach should make this explicit — the injury is not what you didn't do, it's what you didn't say. This is the most precise finding in the series for the magnanimous but unflinching voice — PRV3's content register: direct and confident, without being punitive.
Single-loop vs. double-loop learning explains the most frustrating organizational pattern
Organizations that know they have a problem and cannot resolve it are almost always applying single-loop solutions to double-loop problems. They keep implementing better versions of the wrong approach. The Learning Architecture Failure condition and the Argyris and Schön framework give the PRV3 diagnostic the precision to name this specifically — which is the most valuable thing the private output can do for a leader who has already tried and failed to fix the condition.
Five conditions are now confirmed by four or more independent experiments
AI Governance Failure, Performative Equity, The Inclusion Gap, Organizational Deafness, and Psychological Safety Collapse have been confirmed independently by tool analysis, Glassdoor reviews, exit interviews, conference themes, litigation data, insurance underwriting, and organizational psychology research. These five conditions have the strongest multi-methodology validation in the taxonomy. They should be the first to receive complete dimensional profiles, severity specifications, and financial consequence narratives for the PRV3 output architecture.
