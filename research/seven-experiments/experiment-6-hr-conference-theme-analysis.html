Theme Cluster
AI in HR — Governance, Bias, and the Human Question
Trajectory
Marginal → Dominant
Conferences
All five, 2023–2025
New Candidates
2
What Practitioners Are Worried About
The governance gap. Organizations are deploying AI in hiring, performance, compensation, and workforce planning faster than they can assess the bias, transparency, and liability implications. The sessions that fill rooms aren't "how to use AI" — they're "how to govern AI you've already deployed" and "what to do when your AI hiring tool gets you sued."
Condition Underneath
AI Governance Failure — the organization has deployed AI-assisted decision tools without understanding the regulatory exposure, bias risks, or explainability requirements those tools create. A specific and time-bounded variant of Regulatory Exposure but distinct in mechanism, exposure type, and resolution path.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
AI Governance Failure
AI decision tools deployed without regulatory compliance, bias assessment, or explainability protocols. NYC Local Law 144, EU AI Act, and EEOC guidance all require what most organizations can't currently provide.
New Candidate
Human Displacement Anxiety
AI deployed without managing the human response. Employees uncertain about role security and value in an AI-augmented environment. Produces disengagement and departure of the people who would have been most effective AI collaborators.

Theme Cluster
Employee Wellbeing as Strategic Imperative
Trajectory
Peaked 2020-21, elevated
2024-25 Focus
Measurement and ROI
New Candidates
2
What Practitioners Are Worried About
Return on wellbeing. Organizations have significantly increased wellbeing spending without a measurement framework that demonstrates impact. The sessions that draw most attendance are about measurement — how to know whether wellbeing programs work and how to defend the investment to a CFO who wants to cut it.
Condition Underneath
Wellbeing Theater — visible investments in wellbeing programs without addressing the organizational conditions producing the wellbeing problem. Mental health apps for employees whose managers create the conditions requiring intervention. Financial wellness programs in organizations that pay below market. The investment is real. The impact isn't.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Wellbeing Theater
Structural mismatch between wellbeing investment and wellbeing conditions. A specific variant of Culture Drift — the stated value and the structural reality have diverged at the wellbeing dimension specifically.
New Candidate
Purpose Deficit
The organization can't answer whether its existence makes things better or worse — and younger employees are asking. Distinct from engagement (relationship to role) and culture (behavioral norms). About the organization's relationship to its own reason for existing.

Theme Cluster
DEI — From Commitment to Accountability
Trajectory
Peak 2020-21, deeper 2022-25
2024-25 Focus
Why it isn't working
New Candidates
1 + 2 confirmed
What Practitioners Are Worried About
The gap between commitment and outcome. Organizations that made public DEI commitments in 2020 are now five years in with measurably little to show in leadership representation. The sessions drawing most attendance in 2023-25 are specifically about why diverse talent is hired but not advanced, and why DEI training produces no behavior change.
Condition Underneath
The accountability architecture for DEI commitments is absent. Commitments were made. The measurement framework, consequences for non-performance, and governance structure holding anyone responsible were never built. This is distinct from Performative Equity — it's specifically about the absence of consequences for DEI non-performance.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
DEI Accountability Collapse
DEI commitments exist. The accountability infrastructure to enforce them doesn't. Leaders who don't advance diverse talent face no consequences for the gap. A governance condition, not a culture condition.
Triple-Confirmed
Performative Equity
Confirmed across Glassdoor, exit interview, and conference data. Three independent experiments pointing to the same condition. High-priority placement in the taxonomy.
Triple-Confirmed
The Inclusion Gap
Diverse talent hired at entry level, filtered out before leadership. Confirmed across all three experiments. Now the strongest cross-validated new candidate in the taxonomy.

Theme Cluster
The Manager Crisis
Trajectory
Consistent and increasing
2024-25 Focus
Why development isn't working
New Candidates
3
What Practitioners Are Worried About
Manager development investment isn't producing manager effectiveness. Organizations are spending more on manager training than at any previous point. Engagement scores attributed to manager quality aren't moving. The question has shifted from "how do we develop managers" to "why doesn't development change behavior."
Condition Underneath
Training-Behavior Gap — manager development investment is delivered but doesn't translate into changed behavior. The consistent upstream cause: managers are trained in isolation from the organizational systems governing their behavior. A manager trained in feedback doesn't give better feedback if their performance review doesn't measure it, their span of control doesn't give them time for it, and their own manager doesn't model it.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Training-Behavior Gap
Development investment is delivered; behavior doesn't change. The organizational systems governing manager behavior haven't changed alongside the training. The investment is real. The environment rewards the old behavior.
New Candidate
Manager Accountability Vacuum
Managers are developed without being held accountable for the outcomes of their management. Training measured by completion. Manager effectiveness measured by the manager's output, not the team's experience. An extension of The Untouchable to the middle manager layer.
New Candidate — High Priority
The Squeeze
Middle managers responsible simultaneously for their own individual contribution, team performance, and upward strategy communication — without adequate time, support, or authority for any of the three. The Overloaded Manager at cultural and systemic scale. One of the highest-frequency conditions in the current landscape.

Emerging 2021 → Dominant

What Practitioners Are Worried About
The transition is harder than it sounds and most organizations are stuck in the middle. They've announced a skills-based approach, started mapping skills taxonomies, and discovered their existing systems — HRIS, performance management, compensation architecture — weren't designed for skills-based operation. The middle state is worse than either end.
Condition Underneath
Transition Paralysis — the organization has begun a significant talent management transformation and is stuck in the middle state where the old system no longer works reliably and the new system isn't operational. Distinct from Change Absorption Failure — the culture is willing. The infrastructure can't support the change yet.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Transition Paralysis
Significant talent management transformation begun and stuck in middle state. Old system partially dismantled; new system not yet functional. High current frequency — most large organizations are somewhere in this middle state.
Confirms Existing Candidate
Skills Visibility Gap
Confirmed across tool and conference experiments. Skills inventories built from job descriptions rather than demonstrated capability. The map is fiction.

Theme Cluster
Pay Transparency and Equity
Trajectory
Regulatory-driven, 2022+
Driver
State legislation
New Candidates
2
What Practitioners Are Worried About
Implementation. Most organizations don't know what pay transparency will reveal about their own compensation practices until they're required to post ranges. The sessions drawing most attendance are about what to do when the ranges reveal compression, inequity, and inconsistency the organization wasn't prepared to address publicly.
Condition Underneath
Transparency Exposure — compensation practices with accumulated inconsistencies that are defensible in a low-transparency environment and indefensible in a high-transparency one. The regulations aren't creating the problem. They're revealing it. A time-bounded variant of Compensation Incoherence produced by regulatory change.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Transparency Exposure
Compensation inconsistencies accumulated over time; now exposed by regulatory requirements. Organizations discovering their pay practices publicly rather than proactively face both the correction cost and the reputational damage simultaneously.
New Candidate — High Currency
Compression Crisis
New hires offered at or above salaries of longer-tenured employees in the same role. Simultaneously losing existing employees who discover the compression and constrained on offers by transparency requirements. A dynamic condition created by intersecting pressures.

Theme Cluster
The Future of Work / Hybrid and Remote
Trajectory
Dominant 2020-22, steady
2024-25 Focus
Conditions hybrid created
New Candidates
2
What Practitioners Are Worried About
The equity and culture problems that hybrid creates rather than solves. Remote employees invisible to promotion decisions. In-person employees who feel culture has been diluted. Managers who can't build team cohesion across distributed arrangements. The sessions that persist in 2024-25 are about organizational conditions hybrid created, not about hybrid itself.
Condition Underneath
Proximity Bias — decisions about advancement, recognition, and opportunity systematically favor physically present employees over remote employees regardless of contribution. A structural Attitude liability — the bias isn't intentional but it's consistent and produces measurable inequity in outcomes.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Proximity Bias
Advancement, recognition, and opportunity systematically favor physically present employees regardless of contribution. Legal dimension: if the remote worker population correlates with protected class status, promotion disparities may create discrimination claims.
New Candidate
Distributed Culture Fragmentation
The organization's culture has fractured along location lines. In-office and remote cultures have diverged to the point of producing different experiences, different leadership relationships, and different career trajectories. Culture Drift applied to a geographic dimension.

Theme Cluster
Workforce Planning in Uncertainty
Trajectory
Steady, elevated 2023-25
Core Problem
Standing, not methodology
New Candidates
2
What Practitioners Are Worried About
They don't have the tools, data, or organizational standing to do strategic workforce planning at the speed and uncertainty level the current environment requires. The sessions drawing most attendance aren't about methodology — they're about how to get leadership to treat workforce planning as strategic input rather than HR administrative exercise.
Condition Underneath
Planning Authority Gap — HR has the capability to do strategic workforce planning and lacks the organizational authority and credibility to have its output treated as strategic input. Distinct from HR Capture (compromised) — this is specifically about the gap between analytical capability and organizational standing to act on the analysis.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Planning Authority Gap
HR has the analytical capability. The organization doesn't treat the output as strategic input. The analysis is done; the decisions are made without it. Extends the HR positioning conditions from the Glassdoor experiment.
New Candidate
Workforce Planning Myopia
Workforce needs planned on a 12-month budget cycle when talent development and market timing requirements operate on a 2-5 year horizon. The planning horizon mismatch means the organization makes long-term people decisions on short-term information — perpetually.

What Practitioners Are Worried About
The question has shifted from "how do we collect employee feedback" to "what do organizations do with it and why is action so rare." The 2023-25 sessions are specifically about the listening-action gap and the self-reinforcing cycle that produces Listening Credibility Collapse.
Condition Underneath
Listening Credibility Collapse — a more severe variant of Organizational Deafness. The organization collected feedback, communicated it was listening, and then did not act. The gap has been visible enough times that employees no longer trust the infrastructure. Survey participation declines. Data reliability deteriorates. The organization's ability to detect its own problems collapses further. Self-reinforcing.
Conditions Surfaced
Extends Existing Candidate
Listening Credibility Collapse
What Organizational Deafness looks like at Endemic severity. The listening infrastructure itself has become the problem — its existence generates cynicism rather than signal. Confirmed across Glassdoor and conference data.

Theme Cluster
Leadership Development ROI
Trajectory
Consistent throughout
Core Frustration
Development doesn't change behavior
New Candidates
1
What Practitioners Are Worried About
The Leadership Development Paradox: organizations invest in leadership development, leaders complete the programs, behavior doesn't change. The 2024-25 sessions identify three upstream conditions that prevent development from producing behavior change — and one of them hadn't been named yet.
Condition Underneath
Development Without Stakes — leadership development programs with no consequences attached to behavior change. Leaders participate because required or because it signals positively. They don't change behavior because nothing in their organizational environment requires it. The learning happens. The application doesn't. Not a program quality problem — a governance problem.
Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Development Without Stakes
Leadership development programs unattached to consequences. Leaders participate; behavior doesn't change because the organizational environment rewards the old behavior. Explains the Training-Behavior Gap at the structural level. An Authority condition — the governance architecture doesn't require behavior change as a development output.
Confirms Existing Candidate
Training-Behavior Gap
Confirmed from the Manager Crisis cluster. The gap exists because development programs have no stakes attached. Same condition, confirmed from a second angle in the same experiment.
Triple-Confirmed Conditions — Validated Across All Three Experiments
Three conditions have now been confirmed independently by the tool analysis, Glassdoor review clustering, exit interview analysis, and HR conference theme analysis. These are the highest-confidence new candidates in the taxonomy: Performative Equity (investment in DEI symbols without systems), The Inclusion Gap (diverse talent hired but not advanced), and Organizational Deafness (listening infrastructure that doesn't produce action). All three deserve high-priority placement in the PRV3 state taxonomy.

Key Findings for PRV3
The conference data confirms a practitioner blindspot the employee data doesn't see
Reviews and exit interviews describe conditions from the employee experience. Conference themes describe what practitioners know is wrong and can't fix. The gap between practitioner knowledge and practitioner capacity to act is itself a condition — Planning Authority Gap and Development Without Stakes are both examples of conditions where the organization knows what's needed and lacks the governance architecture to require it.
The Squeeze is the highest-frequency current condition not yet in the taxonomy
Middle managers structurally set up to fail — responsible for individual contribution, team performance, and strategy communication simultaneously without adequate resources for any of them. This condition is producing The Overloaded Manager at scale, manager attrition, and team performance degradation simultaneously. It's the most prevalent structural condition in the current organizational landscape and it isn't in any existing state.
AI Governance Failure is time-bounded but urgent
Organizations are deploying AI tools at a pace that significantly outstrips their governance infrastructure. The regulatory environment is catching up — NYC, EU, and EEOC guidance are all moving simultaneously. The window between deployment and regulatory consequence is narrowing. This condition has a specific and well-documented financial consequence narrative that earns its place in the PRV3 taxonomy despite being relatively new.
Three experiments have now independently confirmed the same conditions
Performative Equity, The Inclusion Gap, and Organizational Deafness have been confirmed by all three experiments from three completely different angles — employer reviews, exit interviews, and practitioner conference content. Triple confirmation by independent methodologies is the strongest validation the taxonomy process can produce. These three conditions should be among the first to receive complete dimensional profiles and severity specifications.
