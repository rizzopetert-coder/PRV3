Principal Resolution — PRV3 Research // Experiment 5 of 7

Insurance Claims
& Risk Data

The actuarial lens. Where litigation data tells you what happened after the organization failed, insurance data tells you what the industry has calculated will happen — probability, frequency, and severity assessed across thousands of organizations and millions of claim events. Actuaries don't have opinions. They have loss ratios.

If a risk category exists in the underwriting framework, it's because enough organizations with that condition produced enough losses to make the category financially meaningful. Insurance underwriting criteria are the most forward-looking diagnostic lens in the series — they identify conditions the organization has now that will produce claims later.

Actuarial Significance

The factors producing the highest premium loads are confirmed predictors of claim frequency and severity — not assumptions, but observed correlations across thousands of organizations. What underwriters charge for, organizations have proven they should be charged for.

Actuarial Significance
Workers comp actuaries have established through large-sample claims analysis that injury rates correlate with management quality. Organizations with lower manager effectiveness scores have higher injury rates — not because managers cause physical injuries, but because management quality affects the organizational culture around safety reporting, near-miss documentation, and hazard identification.

Confirms Structural Overload, Culture Driven Burnout, The Squeeze, and Invisible Burnout — all already in the taxonomy. Workers comp data adds specific financial consequences: medical costs, temporary disability payments, permanent disability awards, and premium surcharges that persist for years after the claim.
Significant and growing load — fastest-growing claim category in most states
Manager effectiveness correlation — manager quality scores predict injury rates
Safety Culture Deficit — management culture produces under-reporting of safety concerns and hazards, which produces higher injury rates, which produces higher workers comp costs. Underwriting factor — confirmed predictor of injury frequency across large portfolio

Employee Benefits Liability
EBSA Recovery FY2023
$1.4B for participants
Primary Claim Type
Administration errors
Key Regulations
ERISA, ACA, COBRA
Actuarial Significance
Benefits liability claims cluster almost exclusively in organizations where benefits administration complexity exceeds administrative capability. The DOL EBSA recovered $1.4 billion for plan participants in FY2023 — largely from administration errors rather than intentional violations. The condition is structural mismatch, not bad faith.
New Conditions Surfaced
New Candidate
Benefits Administration Gap
The complexity of benefit plans offered exceeds the administrative capability of the people managing them. A technical domain variant of Aptitude — Absence. ERISA, ACA, and COBRA obligations missed not from indifference but from capability mismatch.
New Candidate
Open Enrollment Failure
The Uninitiated applied to benefits administration. A foreseeable high-stakes annual event — open enrollment — occurring without adequate communication, system infrastructure, or administrative support. Produces ERISA liability for enrollment errors, employee harm from coverage gaps, and state insurance regulatory exposure.

Cross-Line Actuarial Findings — What Insurance Data Reveals That Litigation Data Doesn't
Frequency before severity
Litigation data captures claims that reached resolution — large enough or clear enough to pursue. Insurance data captures the full distribution including small claims, settled claims, and near-misses. Policy Currency Gap, Recidivism Risk, and Benefits Administration Gap all produce frequent small losses individually not worth litigating but collectively representing significant organizational cost. The actuarial view is more complete.
Leading indicators before outcomes
Underwriters price risk based on organizational characteristics that precede claims — not on claims already occurred. The factors that predict EPLI claims (handbook currency, training frequency, turnover rate) are organizational conditions that exist before any claim is filed. Insurance underwriting criteria are the most forward-looking diagnostic lens in the series — they identify conditions the organization has now that will produce claims later.
Premium cost as certain financial consequence
Every organizational condition in insurance underwriting criteria has a premium cost attached — not just a potential claim cost. An organization with inadequate supervisor training pays elevated EPLI premiums every year, regardless of whether a claim materializes. The ongoing premium cost of organizational risk conditions is part of the Patterned Consequence Logic (the engine framework linking organizational conditions to quantified financial outcomes) — not just the potential claim value, but the certain annual premium cost the condition produces.

Key Findings for PRV3
Insurance adds the certain annual cost to the potential claim cost
Previous experiments produced potential financial consequences — what a claim might cost when it materializes. Insurance data produces certain annual financial consequences — what the condition costs in elevated premiums every year, regardless of whether a claim materializes. The Patterned Consequence Logic should include both: the ongoing premium cost the condition produces annually and the claim cost it produces eventually. The combination makes the cost-of-inaction case more complete than either alone.
Recidivism Risk is the most actionable finding for PRV3's core use case
An organization that has had a prior EPLI claim and not addressed the root condition is the highest-risk organization in the underwriting portfolio. It's also exactly the organization that Principal Resolution is most likely to engage with — an organization that has already experienced a consequence and hasn't resolved the condition underneath. The private output for Recidivism Risk should be the most direct in the taxonomy: you have already paid for this condition once. You are paying again in your current premium. The next payment will be larger.
AI conditions are now confirmed across four independent experiments
AI Governance Failure and Algorithmic Accountability Gap have been confirmed by the tool experiment, the conference experiment, the litigation experiment, and now the insurance experiment. Four independent methodologies pointing to the same conditions. This is the strongest cross-validation the series has produced for any single new candidate. Both conditions should be prioritized for full dimensional profiling and severity specification.
Turnover rate is an actuarially confirmed risk signal — not just an operational cost
The Attrition as Risk Signal finding has specific implications for PRV3's financial consequence architecture. Organizations should understand that above-benchmark voluntary turnover doesn't just cost them replacement costs and lost productivity — it costs them in EPLI premium every year, because underwriters have confirmed that the conditions producing the attrition are the same conditions producing the claims. The turnover rate is a leading indicator for EPLI exposure. This is a financial consequence narrative that no other consulting tool has made explicit.
