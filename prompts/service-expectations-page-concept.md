# Service Expectations Page — Concept Sketch

Status: RAW IDEA, just captured. Not scoped, not approved for build, not yet connected to any
specific page or URL. This file exists so the idea survives context loss — nothing here is
locked.

## Origin
Pete's own framing, verbatim in spirit: a section of the site devoted to clarity about service
expectations. Five components:
- What we guarantee.
- What we stand behind but cannot guarantee.
- What we commit to.
- What we actually do.
- How to resolve disagreements.

## Why this matters, and how it connects to existing findings
This is a sharper, more specific instance of the site-wide orientation gap already logged this
session (Priority Queue item 14, MOB Decision Register): that finding was about visitors not
being oriented to what the diagnostic *is* before engaging with it (homepage's undifferentiated
three-path split, `/about` being an unbuilt stub). This concept applies the same underlying
problem — the instrument/practice not explaining itself before asking for engagement — to the
commercial relationship itself: what a client can actually expect once they're working with
Principal Resolution, not just what the diagnostic tells them.

Also resonates with P-06 ("the instrument meets the user where they are, cannot assume prior
orienting work") at yet another altitude — this session already applied that principle to a
single component (ConstellationField's interpretability), then to the homepage/about gap, and
now potentially to the entire service relationship.

## The five components, as stated — worth distinguishing as genuinely different claims
Worth flagging early, since these aren't just five bullet points of the same thing — they read
as staking out real, different categories of commitment with real legal/trust weight:
- **Guarantees** — the strongest claim, presumably rare and specific.
- **Stand behind but cannot guarantee** — a real, honest middle category; this is often the
  hardest one to write well, since it requires naming the actual limits of confidence.
- **Commitments** — process/behavior promises, not outcome promises.
- **What we actually do** — plain description of the work itself, separate from any promise
  about it.
- **Dispute resolution** — process for when expectations and reality diverge.

This shape itself (naming what's guaranteed vs. not, rather than blurring the two) is consistent
with this project's "magnanimous but unflinching" core reframe and the general house style of
being direct rather than hedging everything into vagueness.
